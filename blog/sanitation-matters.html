Why Sanitation Matters More Than You Think

August 5, 2026 • 6 min read • By Paws United Team

We’ve all seen it: a perfectly good stretch of grass or a favorite trail dotted with dog waste. Most of us feel a flash of irritation. But the problem goes far deeper than aesthetics.

The Hidden Health Risks

Dog feces can contain a range of pathogens, including:

  • Roundworms and hookworms – eggs can survive in soil for months and infect children who play in the area.
  • Giardia and Cryptosporidium – cause gastrointestinal illness in both dogs and humans.
  • Bacteria such as E. coli and Salmonella – especially concerning near water sources or playgrounds.

When waste is left behind, these organisms enter the soil and can be tracked into homes on shoes or paws. Rain then washes them into storm drains and local waterways.

Environmental Impact

Unlike wildlife droppings, dog waste is concentrated and often deposited in high-traffic human areas. It adds excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus) that fuel harmful algal blooms in lakes and rivers. These blooms reduce oxygen levels and can kill fish and other aquatic life.

The Social Contract of Dog Ownership

Every time an owner leaves waste, it chips away at the goodwill that allows dogs access to parks, beaches, and trails. Communities that repeatedly deal with unclean spaces often respond with stricter leash laws, reduced off-leash areas, or outright bans. Responsible owners pay the price for the few who don’t clean up.

What Responsible Owners Do

The solution is simple and non-negotiable:

  • Carry bags on every single walk — even short ones.
  • Pick up immediately, rain or shine, day or night.
  • Dispose of bags in proper bins or take them home.
  • Never leave bags on the ground “to collect later.”

At Paws United we treat sanitation as one of our core values. Clean parks keep dogs welcome. Clean parks keep communities healthy. And clean parks make every walk more enjoyable for everyone.

Next time you head out the door, check that you have bags. It’s the smallest habit with the biggest impact.
